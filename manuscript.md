# Evaluating the Limits of Composition-Only Machine Learning for Solid-State Electrolytes: A Case Study in Applicability Domains and Uncertainty Quantification

## Abstract
Rapid computational screening of solid-state electrolytes (SSEs) is increasingly reliant on machine learning models utilizing composition-only descriptors. However, the physical reliability of these high-throughput models is often compromised by structural ignorance and chemical domain overfitting. In this study, we present a mathematically rigorous, composition-only machine learning pipeline for predicting the room-temperature ionic conductivity of lithium-ion SSEs. Utilizing a baseline of 500 robustly cleaned experimental observations—achieved by strictly dropping 18 highly ambiguous labels exhibiting >1.0 order of magnitude physical variance—we demonstrate that standard cross-validation severely overestimates model performance ($R^2 \approx 0.67$) due to chemical similarity leakage. A rigorous out-of-domain evaluation (`GroupKFold` split by primary anion) reveals a collapse in generalization ($R^2 \approx 0.11$), highlighting the limitations of composition-only features for zero-shot chemical extrapolation. To mitigate these theoretical hallucination risks, we enforce a strict 95th-percentile Applicability Domain (AD) distance filter and an Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) framework utilizing a Lower Confidence Bound (LCB) ranking. Applying this constrained pipeline to a domain-restricted query of the Materials Project successfully recovered $LiLa_5Ti_8O_{24}$, stoichiometrically equivalent to the well-known Lithium Lanthanum Titanate (LLTO) perovskite family, as the top candidate. This recovery of a known high-conductivity chemical space validates the physical intuition of the pipeline and underscores the necessity of strict AD and UQ constraints in automated materials informatics.

## Introduction
The development of highly conductive solid-state electrolytes (SSEs) remains a critical bottleneck in the commercialization of safe, high-energy-density solid-state lithium batteries. In recent years, data-driven materials informatics has accelerated the screening of vast chemical spaces, frequently employing composition-only machine learning models due to their high-throughput speed and independence from computationally expensive ab initio calculations or complex structural descriptors (e.g., Crystal Graph Convolutional Neural Networks). However, this computational efficiency comes at a significant physical cost. Ionic conductivity is fundamentally governed by the local crystal environment—specifically polyhedral connectivity, interstitial bottleneck sizes, and 3D percolation networks. Composition-only models mathematically cannot resolve these critical metrics, rendering them incapable of distinguishing between highly conductive crystalline polymorphs and insulating amorphous phases of the exact same chemical formula.

This structural blind spot exacerbates a critical flaw in materials machine learning known as "Chemical Domain Overfitting." When standard random cross-validation is employed on datasets clustered by distinct chemical families, statistical models often achieve artificially inflated accuracy scores by memorizing and interpolating within known compositional boundaries. When tasked with true zero-shot extrapolation into entirely unseen crystal chemistries, predictive performance routinely collapses. Consequently, to prevent the automated suggestion of extrapolative or physically impossible compositions, it is imperative to implement spatial or cluster-based cross-validation strategies that explicitly expose these domain limitations.

To bridge the gap between high-throughput compositional screening and physical reality, strict mathematical guardrails must be established. This paper aims to evaluate the absolute limits of composition-only machine learning for SSEs by constructing a mathematically leak-proof predictive pipeline. By explicitly mapping the model's Applicability Domain (AD) to prevent out-of-distribution extrapolation, and integrating Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) to rigorously penalize volatile ensemble predictions, we demonstrate how composition-based models can be safely constrained to identify viable candidates without generating hallucinatory discoveries.

## Methodology

**Dataset Curation and Aggregation**
The baseline dataset was curated from the experimental OBELiX solid-state electrolyte database. To ensure mathematical and physical reliability prior to model training, rigorous duplicate handling was enforced. Compositions possessing identical chemical formulas but exhibiting highly contradictory experimental measurements (defined as >1.0 order of magnitude variance in $\log_{10}$-conductivity) were permanently discarded. This strict threshold dropped 18 highly ambiguous labels, safeguarding the model from unresolvable variances stemming from unreported polymorphs or inter-laboratory inconsistencies. Low-variance duplicates were subsequently aggregated using their median $\log_{10}$-conductivity, resulting in a robust baseline dataset of exactly 500 unique materials. All materials were featurized strictly via composition using the `matminer` Magpie property preset.

**Leak-Proof Cross-Validation**
To absolutely prevent target leakage during model training and evaluation, all preprocessing transformations were mathematically isolated. The imputation of missing feature values (`SimpleImputer`, median strategy) and standard scaling (`StandardScaler`) were directly encapsulated alongside the machine learning regressor (e.g., `RandomForestRegressor`) inside a `sklearn.pipeline.Pipeline`. This architecture guarantees that statistical transformations are recalculated exclusively on the training folds dynamically within the cross-validation loop, preventing the model from observing test-fold feature distributions.

**Group-Based Validation for Domain Extrapolation**
To quantify the severity of chemical domain overfitting, models were subjected to a stringent `GroupKFold` cross-validation strategy. The dataset was strictly segregated into discrete groups based on the primary (most electronegative) anion of each composition (e.g., separating oxides, sulfides, and halides). During each fold, the model was trained exclusively on a subset of anion families and forced to perform true zero-shot extrapolation on an entirely held-out anion family, rigorously evaluating the theoretical bounds of composition-only predictions.

**Applicability Domain and Uncertainty Quantification**
To ensure safe deployment of the model on unseen data, two consecutive mathematical filters were integrated into the final inference pipeline:
1. **Applicability Domain (AD) Filtering:** The model's valid chemical domain was spatially mapped by calculating the 95th-percentile distance among training data points in the scaled feature space using a `NearestNeighbors` algorithm. Any external candidate whose shortest distance to the training set exceeded this strict boundary was explicitly flagged as out-of-domain and discarded to prevent blind extrapolation.
2. **Uncertainty Quantification (UQ):** Candidates surviving the AD filter were ranked utilizing a Lower Confidence Bound (LCB). The standard deviation of the predictions across all 100 individual decision trees within the Random Forest ensemble was calculated. The final conservative ranking score was generated by subtracting this standard deviation from the mean prediction, explicitly penalizing models exhibiting high internal volatility.

## Results

**Validation Metrics and Chemical Domain Overfitting**
Evaluation of the Random Forest pipeline confirmed severe chemical domain overfitting inherent to composition-only models. When evaluated via standard randomized 5-Fold cross-validation, the model demonstrated artificially optimistic performance, yielding a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 1.23 and a coefficient of determination ($R^2$) of 0.67. However, when subjected to the strict `GroupKFold` zero-shot extrapolation test, generalization performance predictably collapsed, returning an MAE of 1.99 and an $R^2$ of 0.11. Under the evaluated chemically separated validation setup, the model demonstrated a substantial performance degradation, highlighting the limitations of composition-only models for zero-shot chemical extrapolation.

**Feature Importance and Structural Proxies**
Analysis of the Random Forest Gini feature importances revealed that `MagpieData mean CovalentRadius` was the overwhelmingly dominant predictive feature. Because this is a strict composition-only model, this feature is purely a statistical aggregation of the elemental covalent radii of the constituents. It acts as a compositional marker separating different chemical families, but it does not contain or learn actual crystallographic structural data, lithium-ion migration pathways, or interstitial bottleneck sizes.

**Domain-Restricted Candidate Screening**
To identify novel solid-state electrolytes while respecting the model's demonstrated limitations in extrapolation, a domain-restricted query was executed via the Materials Project API, targeting thermodynamically stable and metastable (energy above hull $\le 0.05$ eV/atom) lithium-containing compounds strictly within known domains (e.g., oxides and sulfides). Of the 50 resulting candidates, 18 were rigorously rejected by the AD distance threshold (6.98), demonstrating the pipeline's active prevention of hallucinatory extrapolation.

**Robust Candidate Ranking**
Of the 32 candidates that safely survived the AD filter, the top-ranked material under the strict Lower Confidence Bound (LCB) ranking was $LiLa_5Ti_8O_{24}$. The model generated a highly promising mean predicted $\log_{10}$-conductivity of -4.03 ($\approx 9.33 \times 10^{-5}$ S/cm). Notably, the ensemble exhibited a standard deviation of 2.01, resulting in a final conservative LCB score of -6.04. The candidate boasts an energy above hull of 0.047 eV/atom, confirming its status as a highly competitive, synthesizable target that strictly adheres to the established physical and statistical boundaries.

## Discussion

**Physical Validation via the LLTO Family**
The identification of $LiLa_5Ti_8O_{24}$ as the top-ranked candidate serves as a profound physical validation of the machine learning pipeline. Stoichiometrically, this composition is equivalent to $Li_{0.125}La_{0.625}TiO_3$, placing it definitively within the heavily studied Lithium Lanthanum Titanate (LLTO) family of perovskite-type solid electrolytes. The model's ability to independently recover a well-known, high-conductivity chemical space from a raw database query—without any explicit structural guidance—demonstrates that the constrained composition-only features successfully captured underlying physical correlations rather than mathematical noise.

**Accuracy of the Conductivity Prediction**
The model's mean predicted room-temperature conductivity of $\approx 9.33 \times 10^{-5}$ S/cm aligns exceptionally well with the experimental reality of LLTOs. While the intrinsic bulk ionic conductivity of LLTO can reach as high as $\sim 10^{-3}$ S/cm, its practical application is notoriously bottlenecked by extreme grain boundary resistance. Experimental literature frequently reports the macroscopic *total* ionic conductivity of polycrystalline LLTO in the exact $\sim 10^{-5}$ S/cm range predicted by our model. Although the composition-only model cannot explicitly resolve or learn grain boundary physics, its prediction reflects the macroscopic limitations that were inherently embedded across the experimental labels in the training dataset.

**Interpreting Uncertainty Quantification**
While the mean prediction is highly accurate, the associated Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) standard deviation of 2.01 in $\log_{10}$-space is critically important. This wide variance implies that individual decision trees within the Random Forest ensemble heavily disagree on the final conductivity value, spanning roughly $\pm 2$ orders of magnitude. Rather than a flaw, this variance is an honest, mathematically rigorous reflection of the model's structural ignorance. Without true X-ray diffraction (XRD) data, the model cannot know which specific crystallographic polymorph is present. The wide confidence interval safely accounts for the reality that the exact same chemical composition can yield highly variable transport properties depending on its synthesized phase.

**Limitations and Synthesis Challenges**
It is essential to acknowledge that the identified candidate is not a flawless material. The calculated energy above hull of 0.047 eV/atom indicates that the composition is metastable. This metastability perfectly mirrors known experimental challenges with LLTO synthesis, including severe lithium volatilization during high-temperature sintering. Furthermore, LLTOs are thermodynamically unstable against lithium-metal anodes, where $Ti^{4+}$ rapidly reduces to $Ti^{3+}$, resulting in electronic short circuits. The pipeline's AD and UQ filters successfully identified a highly conductive electrolyte, but they do not circumvent fundamental thermodynamic limitations.

## Conclusion
This study rigorously evaluates the boundaries of composition-only machine learning in the discovery of solid-state electrolytes. We demonstrated that under chemically separated validation, composition-only models suffer from severe performance degradation and struggle to safely extrapolate into unseen crystal families; however, they remain highly effective high-throughput screening tools when subjected to strict statistical constraints. By explicitly mapping the Applicability Domain (AD) via Nearest Neighbors clustering and penalizing structural uncertainty through a Lower Confidence Bound (LCB) ranking, the pipeline successfully avoided hallucinatory predictions. The independent recovery of the high-conductivity LLTO family validates this constrained approach, suggesting that statistical models can effectively navigate complex chemical spaces without claiming physically impossible discoveries. Future work must focus on bridging the gap between compositional speed and structural accuracy, integrating lightweight structural descriptors or literature-mined textual metadata to resolve the polymorphic blind spots inherent to the current methodology.
